Migration Accelerator — The Story

What a client is actually up against, how this offering resolves it, what Business Development and Industry Leaders need to turn that into a specific pitch, and where the story runs ahead of what we've proven.

Every enterprise migration starts the same way: a platform nobody fully trusts anymore, a decade of campaigns nobody fully remembers building, and a go-live date that arrives before anyone finishes counting what's actually there.

That's the moment this offering exists for. Not the migration itself, the part before it: the months normally spent just finding out what you have, done in an afternoon instead, so the real decisions, what moves, what changes, what finally gets retired, get made on evidence instead of institutional memory and best guesses.

This is that story, what it's built on, and how to put it to work.

Here's how it actually goes.
The mechanism

How Migration Accelerator resolves it

Point it at the platform in question, Eloqua or Salesforce Marketing Cloud, and it reads the whole environment directly: every campaign, every asset, every dependency between them. What used to take an analyst weeks of manual documentation now runs in about an hour, and what comes out the other end isn't a guess dressed up as an estimate. It's an audited health score, built from what's actually there.

From there, a person still makes the calls that need a person: keep this, rework that, retire the rest. But every decision is logged, and picking one thing pulls in everything it actually depends on, so a plan can't quietly ship a destination that's missing a shared asset it needed to work. The platform drafts a real build requirement for each object, the kind an engineer can start from rather than a summary they'd have to reverse-engineer.

And for the pieces it's confident about, it doesn't stop at the plan. It previews the change with a dry run, executes it live against the target platform, and keeps a rollback ready in case anything doesn't land clean. That piece is new, and it's the difference between handing a client a plan and finishing the job.

The capabilities

What's actually in the box

Everything above breaks down into a short list of concrete pieces. Worth naming them plainly, because Business Development and Industry Leaders will need to know exactly what's provable and what's still catching up to the story:

  • Source ingestion and automatic inventory for Eloqua and Salesforce Marketing Cloud (Content Builder)
  • An automated audit, a health score plus an exportable report, generated on demandnew
  • Audited disposition decisions, port, rework, or retire, on every object, with full history
  • Dependency-aware destination planning: assigning one object pulls in everything it needs
  • AI-drafted, human-reviewed build requirements per object, down to Journey Builder and SFMC Next specs
  • Dry-run, live execution, and rollback for supported objects, with a verification pass once the run finishesnew
One more, off to the side: a design-to-email pipeline that takes an approved Figma layout and turns it into a built, publishable email, no separate rebuild required. It doesn't fit neatly into the migration story, but it's real, and worth knowing about.
The client profile

Who this fits

The clients where this lands hardest are enterprise accounts migrating between major marketing automation platforms, carrying a sizable, under-documented environment, somewhere in the range of 500 to 2,000-plus objects. That's exactly where the old way of doing this hurts most, and exactly where the gap between our answer and a generalist's guess is largest.

One honest boundary, worth stating plainly: clients don't log into the platform themselves yet. They receive the reports, and review the decisions, in working sessions with us. This is a delivery and presales instrument first. A client-facing portal is a real possibility, not a promise we've made yet.

Which is where Business Development and Industry Leaders come in.
For Business Development and Industry Leaders

Putting it to work

None of the above is a separate pitch for Business Development and Industry Leaders to invent. It's the pitch, full stop, the same story a client hears. What the team needs isn't a different version of it, it's fluency in this one, plus the tools to make it specific to whoever's actually in the room.

That's what the customizer is for. Fill in a client's name and industry, and the story above reframes itself around their real business problem and their real stakes, before it ever reaches an inbox. Presales can run a prospect's raw export through the platform ahead of a scoping call and walk in with a real health score instead of a rough guess. Delivery can run the full engagement through it end to end, cutting analyst hours without cutting rigor. And in a pitch, this is the live proof: a real environment turning into a real plan, and where it applies, a real dry run, in front of the client instead of described to them afterward.

The old pitch
  • A promise to figure out scope after signature
  • An estimate built on intuition and client-reported counts
  • A plan that ends in a manual handoff to engineering
This pitch
  • A real health score, produced live, in the room
  • Scope set from the actual extracted environment
  • A dry run and, where it applies, a finished cutover

The one thing worth being careful about: live execution and rollback are the newest part of the platform, and coverage isn't complete for every object type yet. Don't promise a client live execution on an object type nobody on the team has actually run it against. Check coverage with delivery before that line goes into a proposal.

FAQ

The questions this needs to survive

Does it let us run a migration with fewer people?Yes

The AI drafts the first pass, inventory, descriptions, build requirements, and a person reviews and refines instead of starting from nothing.

Does it let us do it faster?Yes

Assessment is designed to run in about an hour instead of weeks; a full plan for a mid-size environment is designed to fit inside a single sprint.

Can it actually execute the migration, or just plan it?Yes, for supported objects

Dry run first, then live, with rollback and a verification pass after. This is the newest capability, so coverage isn't complete yet, check with delivery before promising the full cutover on a specific engagement.

Does it mean fewer defects?Yes

Dependency-aware planning won't let a destination ship without something it needs to run, the exact class of miss that spreadsheet planning lets through routinely.

Does it give us better scoping, less risk?Yes

The health score comes from an automated audit of the real environment, not client-reported counts, which is usually where scoping risk starts.

Does it let us scale without deep platform specialists on every deal?Qualified yes

Platform knowledge, field semantics, translation heuristics, vocabulary, lives in the platform, so someone without years of Eloqua or SFMC experience can operate it credibly. A specialist should still review and approve; this raises the floor, it doesn't replace judgment.

Where we stand
The numbers are targets, not results yet

The speed figures throughout, the hour-long assessment, the single-sprint plan, are design targets grounded in what's actually built, not numbers pulled from a completed, measured engagement. Worth closing that gap, instrumenting time-to-assessment, tracking real outcomes, before they go into anything client-facing.
